Data decoded · September 2026

More activity does not always mean more jobs.

In Redding, a new cancer center is expanding care while hospital staff at another facility seek better working conditions. In Fresno, a manufacturer has proposed adding 28,000 square feet but projects just five more workers. At the Port of Los Angeles, containers moved at a record pace. Expansion, investment and activity are visible. The job gains are much harder to assume.

California lost 9,300 nonfarm payroll jobs in September. Just four of 11 major industries added jobs. August's previously celebrated 39,400-job gain was revised to 23,100.

EDD September 2026 preliminary estimates · Released October 16, 2026

Scene 1 · Where the jobs changed

Health care grew while hospitality lost ground.

Private education and health services gained 8,000 payroll jobs in September, the largest increase. Professional and business services added 4,000. Leisure and hospitality fell 9,600, and manufacturing fell 4,000. Four sectors gained jobs, five lost jobs and two were unchanged.

Redding's September cancer-center opening illustrates investment in care delivery. The Ventana Grill closure in Pismo Beach illustrates why hospitality staff may need rapid-response help. Neither event establishes the cause of the statewide industry change.

Scene 2 · The revised trend

August's rebound was smaller than first reported.

EDD revised August's gain down from 39,400 to 23,100 jobs. September's 9,300-job decline followed that smaller rebound. The state still had 129,300 more jobs than a year earlier, but monthly movement remains uneven.

The Fresno manufacturer illustrates why additional equipment or production space should not be treated as proof of substantial new employment. In Los Angeles, a record 1,042,652 shipping containers (TEUs) moved through the port in September, but container volume does not tell us whether payrolls or hiring increased. Workforce leaders should ask employers about headcount, shifts, occupations and start dates.

Scene 3 · Change the camera

Unemployment held at 5.1%, but fewer residents were working.

California's seasonally adjusted unemployment rate remained 5.1%. Yet the household survey estimated 15,600 fewer employed residents and a 20,900-person decrease in the labor force. The household survey counts residents, while the establishment survey counts payroll positions. Their monthly changes need not match.

Local differences are substantial. September unemployment was 5.1% in Santa Cruz County, 5.5% in Monterey and 5.7% in San Benito. In Imperial County it was 20.8%. Those county rates are not seasonally adjusted and should not be directly compared with the seasonally adjusted statewide series.

Imperial Valley College reportedly reduced its lithium-related training intake as commercial projects were delayed. The practical lesson is to align cohorts with verified demand rather than projections alone.

Scene 4 · The paycheck

Pay is the lagging measure in this release.

The most recent verified California private-sector average hourly earnings observation is $42.54 in August 2026. A comparable September observation was not yet available when this update was checked on October 16. We do not present August pay as September pay or claim a real-wage gain without inflation evidence.

Workforce boards should look beyond the average to entry pay, hours, advancement opportunities and actual openings. Current Joby CNC postings in Marina and NASSCO skilled-trade opportunities in San Diego illustrate why local employer validation matters even as statewide manufacturing employment declines. Postings do not prove jobs were filled.

BLS/FRED hourly earnings series ↗ · NASSCO trades ↗

An expanding business, a busy port and an advertised job are three different things.

For workforce leaders, the next question is not only which sectors are growing. It is which employers can document openings, start dates, needed skills and wages that make training worthwhile.
