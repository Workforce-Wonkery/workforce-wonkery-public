Monthly labor market update · September 2026

The job count dipped. The deeper story depends on where you look.

EDD September 2026 estimates, released October 16, 2026

In Redding, a cancer center opened while nurses at another hospital protested staffing and contracts. In Fresno, a manufacturer planned a large plant expansion but expected only a few additional workers. At the Port of Los Angeles, containers moved at a record pace. Investment, production and staffing are not the same measurement, which is why California's September payroll report deserves a closer look.

September lost 9,300 payroll jobs, following an August increase revised down from 39,400 to 23,100. The monthly survey week included September 12; later openings and announcements are context, not an explanation of that count.

Scene 1: Where the growth is

Health care expands. Hospitality can still lose workers.

Only four of the state's 11 major industry groups gained payroll jobs in September. Private education and health services added 8,000, but leisure and hospitality lost 9,600, the largest monthly sector decline. These are statewide, seasonally adjusted employer estimates, not counts of hires or people displaced at particular businesses.

The North State shows why counting new beds is not enough. Mercy Medical Center Redding opened the $70 million Sierra Pacific Regional Cancer Center, which serves patients from several northern counties. On September 3, nurses at a different Redding hospital, Shasta Regional, held a strike over staffing and contract concerns. One event illustrates expanded care capacity; the other raises questions about retaining workers. Neither tells us how many net health jobs were added locally.

Meanwhile, Pismo Beach's Ventana Grill was due to serve its last customers on September 12 after a court ruling linked to structural concerns. A closure can require rapid worker support even when tourism activity elsewhere looks healthy. One restaurant cannot account for California's 9,600-job hospitality decline.

Scene 2: The revision changes the story

More goods and bigger factories do not always mean more jobs.

EDD cut its estimate of August job growth from 39,400 to 23,100. September then saw a loss of 9,300 jobs. California still had 129,300 more nonfarm payroll jobs than a year earlier, but monthly job creation remains uneven.

Consider two very different regions. A City of Fresno environmental filing for California Natural Color describes a 28,000-square-foot addition and four more dryers over four years, yet projects just five additional employees. At the Port of Los Angeles, September cargo reached a port record 1,042,652 TEUs, while the statewide trade, transportation and utilities industry added only 300 payroll jobs. The port measure covers a different geography and period, so the figures cannot be treated as a direct comparison.

The useful question for workforce boards is not just whether output is expanding. How many workers are actually being hired, into which occupations, on what timetable? Capacity projections and container counts can help frame that discussion, but they cannot replace confirmed demand.

Scene 3: Change the camera

One statewide unemployment rate masks very different regions.

The seasonally adjusted California unemployment rate was 5.1% in September. But a separate household survey found 15,600 fewer employed residents and 20,900 fewer people in the labor force. Payroll employment, employed residents and unemployment measure different things and should not be combined as if they were one count.

EDD's not seasonally adjusted county estimates vary widely: San Francisco 4.0%, Fresno 7.4%, and Imperial 20.8%. They are local views with a different seasonal basis than the statewide trend; their differences do not establish a cause.

In Imperial County, an August investigation found that Imperial Valley College reduced lithium-oriented training from more than 60 students to 24 as commercial projects took longer than promised. The college is broadening the program to other jobs. This does not explain the county unemployment rate, but it illustrates a decision facing training providers: how to prepare early without stranding students when employer demand arrives late.

Scene 4: The paycheck

New employment estimates arrived. September wage data did not.

California's latest independently verified BLS private-sector average hourly earnings observation remains $42.54 in August 2026, about 3.1% higher than August 2025. September earnings are pending in this series. This is a nominal average, not an inflation-adjusted measure or a typical worker's pay.

The quality-of-work question shows up across the state. In Irvine, Hoag opened its expanded Sun Family Campus on September 21, after the employment survey week. Its earlier announcement projected about 1,000 new jobs and committed $30 million toward workforce housing assistance. Those jobs are a forecast, not verified September hiring. They show that an employer preparing to add services may still need help keeping workers able to live nearby.

In San Diego and Marina, shipbuilding and aircraft manufacturers have advertised skilled trade and production roles despite statewide manufacturing job losses. Job advertisements show opportunities to investigate, not accepted offers or actual payroll additions. Workforce boards should validate pay, hours, qualifications and confirmed start dates before committing training funds.

Hoag: Sep. 21 opening · Hoag: housing and projected hires · NASSCO: skilled trades · Joby: CNC role · BLS/FRED August earnings

September's signal is narrower than the headline: slower payroll growth, more uneven regional conditions, and investments that do not always translate into new jobs.

The next workforce-board decision is to check which employers are hiring now, what skill or job-quality problem keeps openings unfilled, and which training promises depend on projects not yet operating.

Sources, definitions, and update notes
  • California EDD September 2026 employment release, published October 16, 2026. Official statewide seasonally adjusted payroll, labor-force and 11-sector industry tables, plus the 58-county unadjusted labor-force table. September payroll jobs: 18,151,600, down 9,300; August revised to 18,160,900, up 23,100 (not the previously estimated 39,400); statewide unemployment 5.1%; civilian labor force declined 20,900.
  • BLS total nonfarm payroll employment (CANA) via FRED. The historical monthly series was accepted September 30, 2026 and may lag newly revised EDD preliminary values. The August 2026 and September 2026 chart observations explicitly use EDD's October 16 release.
  • BLS California unemployment rate (CAUR) via FRED; October 2025 is not available following the 2025 federal shutdown. September 2026 5.1% is directly from EDD's October 16 release.
  • BLS/FRED private-sector average hourly earnings, SMU06000000500000003, not seasonally adjusted. The most recent verified month on October 16 was August 2026 ($42.54); August 2025 was $41.25. The 3.1% increase is nominal, not an estimate of purchasing power. September earnings are pending in this dataset.
  • Hiring breadth counts only major industry sectors whose month-over-month payroll estimates are positive; zero is not a gain. September: four positive, five negative, two flat, out of 11. This is not the number of firms recruiting or open jobs.
  • Hoag, September 21, 2026: opened Sun Family Campus in Irvine with six added buildings and 155 inpatient beds. This is employer-announced facility capacity, not measured September payroll additions.
  • Hoag, February 9, 2026: announced a $30 million workforce-housing commitment and anticipated approximately 1,000 new jobs related to its campus opening. Employment figure is a projection, not verified achieved hiring.
  • Bay Area News Group, September 24, 2026: companies disclosed 1,196 planned permanent Bay Area job cuts as of its reporting date, including Oracle, Uber and PayPal. Announcements are not completed job losses and do not map one-to-one onto EDD industry definitions.
  • Do not substitute September state payroll employment for household civilian employment; do not treat seasonally adjusted state unemployment and unadjusted county labor-force rates as one directly comparable series. September county values were taken from the same EDD release's 2025-benchmark table.
  • This is a dated source-backed snapshot, not a live feed. Historical monthly series may be revised. Occupation, wage and QCEW products keep separate data-vintage labels.
  • The official EDD September 2026 release identifies the September 12 survey reference week. Local events after that week are context only, not proof of September payroll effects.
  • California CEQAnet, filed September 11: California Natural Color / Gallo Fresno expansion plans 28,000 additional square feet and five expected additional workers. The jobs are projected, not realized; construction phases may extend for several years.
  • Port of Los Angeles, October 9: September throughput of 1,042,652 TEUs is an activity measure for one port over the full month, not an employment or job-opening measure.
  • Dignity Health, September 1: Sierra Pacific Regional Cancer Center opened to serve existing regional oncology demand; the announcement is not a net payroll count. Shasta Scout, September 3 reports a staffing and contract strike at a different Redding hospital.

  • SCAG, September 2: Riverside and San Bernardino combined job growth through June 2026 was narrowly concentrated in health care. This is a different period and geography from September statewide estimates.
  • NASSCO and Joby Aviation publish employer-specific openings. Recruitment advertisements are not evidence of completed hires.
  • Fresno Pacific University, September 25: the CalViva nursing simulation lab opened September 23, after the EDD survey week. Its completion, licensure and employment outcomes are not reported by this source.
  • Case-selection principle: use local events to illustrate workforce decisions, not as a sampling method for the monthly employer survey. Source events need a dated report, correct geography, type (actual, projected, announced, modeled or posted), and an explicit evidence limit.
